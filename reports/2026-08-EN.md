# sesport – August 2026

> Draft through August 25.

August has so far been less about building new foundations and more about
making the entire chain safer, clearer, and more automated. This includes
data collection, verification, publishing, and the public experience.

## Key changes

- **Verification became its own part of the workflow.** Rules for daily
  checks, participant evidence, and public sanity checks were documented and
  began guiding which activities are ready to publish.

- **Participant data became more precise.** Roles, disciplines, status, and
  start times are handled more clearly. Inactive participants can be hidden,
  priority participants can be emphasized, and evidence requirements became
  stricter.

- **Broadcasts and activities were connected more tightly.** Importing,
  grouping, and channel handling improved, while broadcast or stream links
  can now follow the data through to the public activity.

- **The public site received substantial polish.** Time display, grouping,
  mobile layouts, date navigation, participant lists, and automatic reloads
  were improved. Member flows and watched activities also began taking shape.

- **Operations became more automated.** AI jobs and post-run checks were tied
  more closely to publishing, with clearer rules for what can happen
  automatically and what still requires caution.

Overall, August has so far been the month when sesport started feeling less
like a system that can produce good data and more like a system that can do so
repeatedly, under control, and in a form that users can understand.

**In short:** the focus shifted from adding features to increasing trust,
automation, and the maturity of the public product.
